## Reading

The correspondence-layer distance decay reported in earlier drafts
(rho = -0.50, p = 0.002) does not survive scrutiny. It was stacked
from three artifacts. (i) Six phantom data points from bad gazetteer
matches - four actors coded to a 'Syria' region point in the Ionian
Sea, Pihilu matched to Macedonian Pella, Irqata to Anatolian Arca -
all far-with-few-letters, exactly the shape that manufactures decay
(Tyre was also mislocated to a homonymous estate in Jordan).
(ii) Spearman without tie-averaging: 14 of 35 actors tie at one
letter, and raw order ranks let input row order leak into rho
(-0.45 -> -0.32 on identical data). (iii) Composition: the residual
trend concentrates in polities with political reasons to write
little (Hatti-aligned Qadesh/Sidon; quasi-independent Ugarit);
without them rho = -0.18, p = 0.32, and single-polity deletion
leaves significance in a minority of variants. The partial effect
of court distance given administrative proximity, coast, and
alignment is -0.26 (p = 0.17); no covariate predicts volume.

Conclusion: the pre-registered RQ5 prediction stands after all -
distance decays mention and conflict ties (both robust to the
coordinate corrections, p < 0.01) while correspondence with the
court shows no defensible distance effect at this n. The sign
stays negative in every variant, so a weak true effect is not
excluded - but nothing here licenses claiming one.
