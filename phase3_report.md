## 6. Interpretation

**The 'small world' is real but construction-dependent and far more
modest than claimed.** Person-to-person co-occurrence clustering is
0.42 — same ballpark as Cline & Cline's 0.391 — and their ER-style
comparison would let us proclaim '14x random'. But cliques manufacture
triangles mechanically: against the bipartite configuration null
(letter sizes and person frequencies preserved), the observed CC of
0.42 stands against a null median of 0.26. The honest sentence is:
*clustering exceeds the fair null by ~1.6x, not 49x* — genuine social
structure, mostly explained by who-appears-in-letters-together.

**Cline & Cline's Rib-Hadda finding replicates under their
construction and dissolves under bias correction.** In the
sender-star + correspondence graph (closest to their hand-coded
object), Rib-Hadda is #2 in betweenness behind only the collapsed
PHARAOH node — and since they *split* the pharaohs (Amenhotep III vs
Akhenaten vs 'the king'), each individual pharaoh falls below him —
section 2c CONFIRMS this: split the pharaohs and Rib-Hadda (4625)
beats Amenhotep IV (3304) and Amenhotep III (1084), exactly their
headline, but only because the residual 'unidentified pharaoh' node
(18208) absorbs most royal traffic. Their most famous finding is an
artifact of splitting one actor's identity across three nodes under
uncertainty. In the person-to-person layer Rib-Hadda is only
#6, and with dossiers equalized at the median (2 letters/sender) he
falls to median rank 13 with P(top-3) = 0.0.

**The structural brokers of the vassal network are Aziru and
Yanhamu.** Aziru of Amurru holds top betweenness in every variant
(P(top-3) = 0.99 even dossier-equalized) — the defector who bridged
Egypt's orbit and Hatti's. Yanhamu, the Egyptian commissioner, rises
to #2 (P(top-3) = 0.86) once volume is corrected — precisely RQ1's
hypothesis that officials, not the loudest vassal, carried the
network. Rib-Hadda's fame is a fact about his archive, not his
position.

**Community structure is real in this layer** (modularity ~0.56, k~35
with ~8 substantive blocks) and the big communities read as political
theatres: the Amurru crisis (Aziru/ʿAbdi-Aširta/Rib-Hadda/Yanhamu),
the Damascus-Qadesh axis (Biryawaza/Etakkama), the Shechem-Jerusalem
conflict (Labaya/ʿAbdi-Heba), the Tyre-Sidon feud (Zimreddi/
Abi-Milku). Phase 4 SBMs will test these against the tier partition.

**Caveats:** EA 1-44 mentions are absent (not yet lemmatized) — the
Great-Power brotherhood is invisible here, which depresses clustering
relative to Cline & Cline's corpus; entity resolution is registry-v1
(one pass, unadjudicated); the bipartite null dedupes within-letter
repeats, slightly shrinking group sizes (conservative direction).
