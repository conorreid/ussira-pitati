The gazetteer audit

How seven phantom coordinates manufactured a finding — a cautionary tale for anyone geocoding the Bronze Age against a classical gazetteer.

An early draft of this project reported something surprising: vassals farther from Egypt sent fewer letters to pharaoh (ρ = −0.50, p = 0.002). It contradicted our own pre-registered prediction — writing to the king was a loyalty obligation, and obligations shouldn’t thin with distance — and it looked like a genuine discovery about how Late Bronze Age empire worked.

It wasn’t. Asking why distance mattered forced someone to finally look at the 39 data points one by one, and the finding dissolved in three stages.

Stage one: the phantoms

Our automated join against the Pleiades gazetteer had matched Bronze Age toponyms to classical homonyms in the wrong places:

  • Four one-letter senders coded to the region “Syria” — whose representative point sits in the Ionian Sea, off Kefalonia;
  • Pihilu (Pella in the Jordan valley) matched to Pella in Macedonia — birthplace of Alexander, 1,600 km from home;
  • Irqata (Tell Arqa, Lebanon) matched to an Arca in Anatolia;
  • Tyre matched to Tyros — a Hellenistic estate in Jordan that happens to share the name;
  • Hatti matched to the Iron-Age Syro-Hittite region near Gaziantep rather than Hattusa;
  • Assyria matched to Leukosyria — a region on the Black Sea coast that carries “Assyria” as a name variant;
  • Alašiya (Cyprus) placed in inland Anatolia — this one is an error in the source itself: Pleiades’ own representative point for place 991392 “Cyprus” lies ~250 km off the island. (Reported upstream.)

All seven pushed the correlation the same way. Correcting them — every coordinate now verified against the Pleiades bulk dump — weakened the effect from ρ = −0.50 to −0.45.

Stage two: the tie bug

Fourteen of the 35 located vassals sent exactly one letter. The Spearman implementation didn’t average ranks across ties, which means arbitrary input order leaked into the statistic — the published number depended on the row order of a CSV file. Fixed, ρ = −0.32, p = 0.07. No longer significant, before any further scrutiny.

Stage three: politics, not geography

What trend remained was carried by polities with political reasons to write little: Qadesh and Sidon had gone over to the Hittite side (EA 174–176, 148–149), and Ugarit was quasi-independent. Without them: ρ = −0.18, p = 0.32. A leave-one-polity-out jackknife kept significance in only 7 of 24 deletions, and in a partialled design against distance-to-nearest-garrison, coastal access, and Hatti alignment, nothing predicted letter volume at all.

The moral

The finding died not because the hypothesis was tested and rejected, but because asking “what would explain this?” forced someone to actually look at the data points. Six of them had been flagged needs_review the whole time. The analysis just never checked the flag — and the seventh error wasn’t flagged at all.

The paper this project produced argues that the famous quantitative claims about the Amarna archive are outputs of unexamined construction choices. This page is the demonstration that the argument applies to everyone — including us. The full audit trail, with every verdict quoted to its source, is in the repository (distance_confounds.md).
